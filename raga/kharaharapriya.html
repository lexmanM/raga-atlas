About Kharaharapriya

Kharaharapriya is melakarta 22 of the 72 parent rāgas in Carnatic music. Ārohana S R2 G2 M1 P D2 N2 Ṡ; avarohana Ṡ N2 D2 P M1 G2 R2 S.

Primer

How rāgas are organised

Five ideas, in plain language. No notation required.

01

An octave has twelve doors

Pick any note and sing until you reach the same note again, higher. Between those two points sit twelve usable pitches — the same twelve on a piano. Carnatic music calls each landing place a svarasthāna, a “note position”.

02

Seven names, sixteen shades

The seven note names are Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Da Ni — written S R G M P D N. Sa is home and Pa never moves. The others have two or three flavours each, marked with a subscript.

03

A rāga is a path, not a scale

A rāga specifies the way up — the ārohana — and the way down — the avarohana. They need not match. That asymmetry is most of a rāga's character.

04

The parent rāgas are a grid, not a list

A melakarta is a parent rāga: all seven names present, straight up and straight down. Two Ma choices × six Ri–Ga pairs × six Da–Ni pairs = 72 parents. These are dealt into twelve groups called chakras.

05

The children are where the music lives

A janya rāga borrows its notes from one parent, then drops some, bends the order, or takes a different route down. Most rāgas you will actually hear sung are janyas; the parents are the filing system that makes them findable.
